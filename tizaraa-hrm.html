Tizaraa HRM — Enterprise Human Resource Management System

A multi-company enterprise HRM platform built with Laravel 10. It manages the full employee lifecycle: onboarding, attendance, leave, shift scheduling, payroll, and HR analytics. Admin, HR, department heads, and employees each get role-based, department-scoped data access.

Period
May 2025 – Present
Stack
Laravel 10 · PHP 8.2 · MySQL

My Role

The system replaced manual, spreadsheet-based HR operations with a centralized, rule-driven platform. I owned the HR modules on the backend.

  • Built the entire payroll system from scratch
  • Owned core HR modules: Employee, Attendance, Leave, Late Apply, Attendance Correction, Shift Roster
  • Designed the database schema and migrations (~260+)
  • Implemented approval workflows and RBAC rules
  • Developed reporting, exports, and document generation
  • Wrote internal technical documentation used by the team

Key Features

Payroll is the core module. Everything else feeds into it.

Payroll Engine
  • Salary structure engine: earnings & deductions with fixed / percentage rules
  • Versioned salary assignments with effective dates (full salary history)
  • Weekly / monthly cycles, department-wise or employee-wise
  • Transaction-safe, idempotent cycle generation
Attendance-Driven Salary
  • Salary calculated from present, leave, absence, and late rules
  • Late deduction policy: 3 lates = 1 deduction day
  • One attendance summary engine shared by payroll, reports, and exports
Payroll Documents
  • Payslip PDF
  • Salary sheet (PDF / Excel)
  • Salary certificates
  • Finance-ready bank disbursement files
HR Lifecycle
  • Employee management with structured profiles
  • Leave, late, and attendance correction workflows
  • Shift roster management
  • Multi-level approval: manager → final approver
  • Full audit trail of all HR actions
Role & Department Access
  • Granular RBAC with 5 permission levels: added, owned, both, all, none
  • Department-scoped access to employee and HR data
  • Parent-department filtering for hierarchical organizations
  • Per-user permission overrides for special cases
Reporting & Export
  • Filter-based Excel exports (leave, attendance, payroll, late requests)
  • Payroll summaries and missing-data reports
  • Leave cards and HR letters (PDF with company letterhead)
  • Attendance and salary breakdown reports
Security & Authentication
  • OTP-based admin login
  • Configurable session expiry for sensitive roles
  • Secure API authentication with Sanctum / Fortify

Key Challenges & Solutions

Problems I ran into while building the HR modules, and how I fixed them.

Accurate, Repeatable Payroll

Payroll must never duplicate or lose records.

Solution: transaction-based cycle generation with idempotent checks, and per-component breakdown storage for full auditability.

Mid-Cycle Salary Changes

Promotions and increments affected historical payroll correctness.

Solution: effective-dated salary assignments, so payroll always reflects the actual salary at the time of service.

Payroll vs Attendance Mismatch

Manual reconciliation created inconsistencies.

Solution: one attendance summary engine shared across payroll, reports, and exports.

Broken Approval Authorization

In the legacy flow, managers could approve outside their scope.

Solution: a new employee_approval_permissions table that separates request routing from final authorization.

Department Data Leakage Risk

The global all permission exposed unintended data.

Solution: a parent-department access layer for scoped visibility, without breaking the existing permission model.

Large Legacy Codebase

200+ controllers made impact analysis difficult.

Solution: technical documentation (routes, schema, HR manual) used as the system reference for safe development.
